XAUUSD Pip Value Explained

How much is 1 pip in gold? XAUUSD pip value explained: points vs pips, $10 per pip per lot on a 100 oz contract, tables and examples.

If you have ever asked "how much is 1 pip in gold?" and received three different answers, you are not alone. The XAUUSD pip value depends on how your broker quotes the price, how many ounces sit in one lot, and which convention the person answering you happens to use. This guide separates points from pips, shows the numbers for the most common contract size, and explains how to check your own broker's specification so you never have to guess.

Points, pips and dollar moves: three different things

Gold is quoted in US dollars per troy ounce. In this guide we use example prices around 4,200, so a quote might read 4,200.35. The smallest step the price can move is called a point (MT5 also calls it a "tick" size in the symbol specification).

  • 2-digit quotes (4,200.35): one point = 0.01.
  • 3-digit quotes (4,200.352): one point = 0.001.

A pip has no official definition for gold. In forex a pip is a fixed decimal place, but gold never had one agreed by everyone. The most widely used convention today is 1 pip = 0.10 of price, so a move from 4,200.00 to 4,200.10 is one pip, and a move from 4,200.00 to 4,201.00 is ten pips. Some traders and some websites instead call 0.01 a pip, and a few call a full $1.00 move a pip. That is the whole reason the answers you find online disagree.

The cleanest way to avoid confusion is to think in dollar moves of price. "Gold moved $1" means the quote moved from, say, 4,200.00 to 4,201.00. Whatever anyone calls a pip, a $1 move is a $1 move.

The formula

Value of a move = lots × contract size (oz) × price change ($)

With the standard contract of 100 ounces per 1.00 lot:

  • A $0.01 move (one point on a 2-digit quote) = 1 × 100 × 0.01 = $1 per lot.
  • A $0.10 move (one pip under the common convention) = 1 × 100 × 0.10 = $10 per lot.
  • A $1.00 move = 1 × 100 × 1.00 = $100 per lot.

So under the 0.10 convention, one pip on gold is worth $10 per standard lot, which conveniently matches the familiar forex figure for EURUSD.

If your broker quotes three decimals, one point is 0.001, worth $0.10 per 1.00 lot. The dollar value of a $0.10 or $1 move does not change; only the size of the smallest step does. If you want to skip the arithmetic, the XAUUSD pip value calculator does it for any lot size.

Why brokers and websites disagree

There are three common sources of disagreement:

  1. Pip definition. One site says a pip is 0.01, another says 0.10. Both may be internally consistent, but their "pip value" numbers differ by a factor of ten.
  2. Digits. A broker that moved from 2-digit to 3-digit quotes makes "points" ten times smaller. A stop of "300 points" means $3.00 on a 2-digit feed and $0.30 on a 3-digit feed.
  3. Contract size. 100 oz per lot is the most common, but some brokers and account types use 10 oz, 1 oz or other sizes, and some offer separate "mini" symbols. With a 10 oz contract, a $1 move on 1.00 lot is worth $10, not $100.
Check before you size a trade. Brokers differ. Never assume 100 oz just because most guides use it. A wrong contract size means your real risk is ten or a hundred times what you planned.

How to check contract size in MT5

MetaTrader 5 shows the exact specification for every symbol your broker offers:

  1. Open the Market Watch window (Ctrl+M).
  2. Right-click XAUUSD (it may carry a suffix such as XAUUSD.m or GOLD).
  3. Choose Specification.
  4. Read Digits, Contract size, Tick size and Tick value.

Tick value is the money value of one tick (one point) for 1.00 lot. On a 2-digit, 100 oz symbol it should read about 1. On a 3-digit, 100 oz symbol it should read about 0.1. If what you see does not match, trust the specification over any article, including this one.

The same applies to automated trading. An Expert Advisor that sizes positions by risk should read tick value and contract size from the symbol at run time rather than hard-coding 100 oz. Worked examples

Example 1. You buy 0.25 lot at 4,200.00 and close at 4,212.50. The price moved $12.50. Result = 0.25 × 100 × 12.50 = $312.50 before spread, commission and swap. In pips (0.10 convention) that is 125 pips at $2.50 per pip for 0.25 lot.
Example 2. You plan a stop $6.00 away from entry on 0.30 lot. Potential loss = 0.30 × 100 × 6.00 = $180.
Example 3. The same 0.30 lot with a broker whose contract is 10 oz: 0.30 × 10 × 6.00 = $18. Same chart, same stop, one tenth of the money. This is why the specification check matters.

Non-USD accounts

XAUUSD profit and loss is calculated in US dollars first, because gold is priced in dollars. If your account is in another currency, the platform converts that dollar figure at the current exchange rate. For example, with an illustrative EURUSD rate of 1.08, a $100 gain becomes about 100 / 1.08 = 92.59 EUR. With a THB or JPY account the same logic applies through USDTHB or USDJPY. This means your pip value in account currency drifts slightly as exchange rates change, even when the gold contract stays the same.

Pip value and margin are not the same thing

Pip value tells you how much you gain or lose per price move. Margin tells you how much of your balance is set aside to open the position. They are related through contract size, but leverage changes margin without changing pip value. If you want the margin side, see how much margin 1 lot of gold needs. Key takeaways

  • A point is the smallest quote step: 0.01 on 2-digit feeds, 0.001 on 3-digit feeds.
  • The common convention is 1 pip = 0.10, worth $10 per 1.00 lot on a 100 oz contract.
  • A $1 move on gold is $100 per 1.00 lot, $10 per 0.10 lot and $1 per 0.01 lot at 100 oz.
  • Brokers differ in digits and contract size, so read the MT5 Specification for your exact symbol.
  • Non-USD accounts see the dollar result converted at the current exchange rate.

FAQ

How much is 1 pip in gold?

Under the most common convention, one pip is a 0.10 move in price, worth $10 per 1.00 lot on a 100 oz contract. Some sources call 0.01 a pip instead, so always confirm which definition is being used.

How much is a $1 move in gold for 0.01 lot?

On a 100 oz contract, 0.01 lot is 1 ounce, so a $1 move is worth $1. Check your broker's contract size, because smaller contracts change this figure.

Why does my broker show 3 decimals on XAUUSD?

Some brokers quote gold to three decimals for finer pricing. One point becomes 0.001 instead of 0.01, but the value of a $1 move is unchanged.

Where do I find the contract size in MT5?

Right-click the symbol in Market Watch and choose Specification. The window lists contract size, digits, tick size and tick value.
