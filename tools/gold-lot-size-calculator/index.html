How much is 1 lot of XAUUSD?

On most brokers 1 standard lot of XAUUSD = 100 troy ounces of gold. So a 1.00 USD move in the gold price changes the value of a 1.00 lot position by 100 USD. At a gold price of 4,200, one lot controls about 420,000 USD of gold (the notional value) - you only post a fraction of that as margin, but your profit and loss is calculated on the full 100 oz.

How the gold lot size formula works

Lot size = (Balance × Risk %) ÷ (Stop distance × Contract size)
  1. Money at risk = balance × risk %. With 1,000 USD and 1% risk you accept losing 10 USD if the stop-loss is hit.
  2. Loss per 1.00 lot = stop-loss distance × contract size. A 10 USD stop on 100 oz = 1,000 USD per lot.
  3. Lot size = money at risk ÷ loss per lot, rounded down to your broker's lot step: 10 ÷ 1,000 = 0.01 lot.

Worked examples

Example 1 - small account. Balance 1,000 USD, risk 1% = 10 USD. Buy at 4,200.00, stop at 4,190.00 (10.00 distance). Loss per lot = 10 × 100 = 1,000 USD. Lot size = 10 ÷ 1,000 = 0.01 lot.
Example 2 - medium account. Balance 5,000 USD, risk 1% = 50 USD. Sell at 4,215.50, stop at 4,227.50 (12.00 distance). Loss per lot = 1,200 USD. 50 ÷ 1,200 = 0.0417 → rounded down to 0.04 lot, a real risk of 48 USD.
Example 3 - stop loss in dollars. You trade 0.10 lot with a 10.00 stop. Loss if hit = 0.10 × 100 × 10 = 100 USD. If you only accept 50 USD, the stop can be at most 50 ÷ (0.10 × 100) = 5.00 away - or keep the stop and trade 0.05 lot.
Too small for the minimum lot? If the result is below 0.01, even the smallest position risks more than you chose. Use a closer stop only if your setup allows it - otherwise skip the trade. Read the 1% rule with gold examples.

FAQ

How much is 1 lot of XAUUSD?

On most brokers 1 lot of XAUUSD is 100 troy ounces of gold, so each 1.00 USD move in price is worth 100 USD per lot. At a price of 4,200 that is about 420,000 USD of gold.

How do I calculate lot size for gold?

Divide the money you are willing to lose by the stop-loss distance times the contract size. For example 10 USD ÷ (10.00 × 100) = 0.01 lot.

How do I calculate the stop loss in dollars on XAUUSD?

Multiply lot size × contract size × stop distance. A 0.10 lot trade with a 10.00 stop on a 100 oz contract loses 0.10 × 100 × 10 = 100 USD if the stop is hit.

What lot size for a 1,000 USD gold account?

At 1% risk you risk 10 USD, so with a 10.00 stop the lot size is 0.01. A wider stop would need less than the minimum lot, which means skipping the trade or choosing a closer, still valid stop.

Is 1 pip on gold 0.01 or 0.10?

It depends on the source. In MetaTrader the smallest step (a point) is usually 0.01, while many traders call 0.10 a pip. The calculator works in price distance, so it is correct either way.
