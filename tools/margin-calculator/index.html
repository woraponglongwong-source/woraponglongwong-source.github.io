How margin is calculated

Margin = Lots × Contract size × Price (in USD) ÷ Leverage

For gold the contract is usually 100 oz, so at a price of 4,200 one lot is worth 420,000 USD (the notional value). With 1:100 leverage the broker holds 420,000 ÷ 100 = 4,200 USD as margin. For pairs where USD is the base currency (USDJPY, USDCHF) the notional is simply lots × 100,000 USD, so price does not matter.

Margin level, margin call and stop out

Margin level % = Equity ÷ Used margin × 100

If equity is 10,000 USD and the trade uses 4,200 USD, the margin level is 238%. When losses push it down to the broker's margin-call level (often 100%) you cannot open new trades; at the stop-out level (often 20–50%) positions are closed automatically. Levels vary by broker.

Worked examples

Gold, small account. 0.05 lot XAUUSD at 4,200 with 1:200 leverage: 0.05 × 100 × 4,200 ÷ 200 = 105 USD margin.
EURUSD. 1 lot at 1.0800 with 1:30: 100,000 × 1.08 ÷ 30 = 3,600 USD.
USDJPY. 0.50 lot with 1:100: 0.5 × 100,000 ÷ 100 = 500 USD (USD is the base currency).
Margin is not risk. Low margin only means the broker lets you open a large position. What you can lose depends on lot size × stop distance - size every trade with the XAUUSD lot size calculator and check its dollar value with the pip value calculator.

FAQ

How much margin do I need for 1 lot of gold?

Lots × 100 oz × price ÷ leverage. At 4,200 that is 4,200 USD with 1:100 leverage, 840 USD with 1:500 and 21,000 USD with 1:20.

How much margin for 0.01 lot of XAUUSD?

At a price of 4,200 a 0.01 lot position is worth 4,200 USD, so it needs 42 USD at 1:100 or 8.40 USD at 1:500.

Does higher leverage increase my risk?

Leverage only changes the margin held. The money you can lose is set by the lot size and the stop-loss distance, but high leverage makes it easy to open positions that are too large.

Why is my broker's margin for gold different?

Many brokers use a separate leverage for metals, a fixed margin per lot, or a margin that changes with position size and around weekends and news. Check the symbol specification in your platform.
