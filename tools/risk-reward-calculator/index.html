How the risk reward ratio works

R:R = |Take-profit − Entry| ÷ |Entry − Stop-loss|
Break-even win rate = 1 ÷ (1 + R)

The ratio compares what you aim to make with what you accept to lose. A trade with a 12.00 stop and a 24.00 target is 1 : 2. At 1 : 2 you only need to be right on more than 1 out of 3 trades to break even before costs; at 1 : 1 you need more than half.

A higher ratio is not automatically better: far targets are reached less often. What matters is the ratio together with how often your method actually reaches the target - which only testing can tell you.

Worked examples

Gold buy. Entry 4,200, stop 4,188 (12.00), target 4,224 (24.00), 0.05 lot. Risk = 0.05 × 100 × 12 = 60 USD, reward = 0.05 × 100 × 24 = 120 USD, R:R 1 : 2, break-even win rate 33.3%.
Gold sell. Entry 4,215, stop 4,223 (8.00), target 4,203 (12.00), 0.10 lot. Risk 80 USD, reward 120 USD, R:R 1 : 1.5, break-even 40%.
With costs. If spread and commission cost 5 USD on the first example, the real reward is 115 and the real risk 65 USD, so R:R drops to 1 : 1.77 and the break-even win rate rises to about 36%.

FAQ

What is a good risk reward ratio for gold?

Many traders look for at least 1 : 1.5 to 1 : 2, but no ratio is good on its own. It has to match how often your strategy reaches the target, including spread and commission.

How do I calculate risk reward?

Divide the distance from entry to take-profit by the distance from entry to stop-loss. A 24.00 target and a 12.00 stop give 2, written 1 : 2.

What win rate do I need with 1:2 risk reward?

Just over 33.3% to break even before costs, because one winner of 2R pays for two losers of 1R.

Does the calculator work for sell trades?

Yes. It detects the direction from the prices: a stop above the entry and a target below it is a sell.
